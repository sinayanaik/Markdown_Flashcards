#### Ashtavakra Gita 1.3

**Original Sanskrit:**
न पृथिवी न जलं नाग्निर्न वायुर्द्यौर्न वा भवान् ।
एषां साक्षिणमात्मानं चिद्रूपं विद्धि मुक्तये ॥३॥

**English Transliteration:**
na pṛthivī na jalaṁ nāgnirna vāyurdyaurna vā bhavān,
eṣāṁ sākṣiṇamātmānaṁ cidrūpaṁ viddhi muktaye. (3)

**Complete Translation:**
You are neither earth, nor water, nor fire, nor air, nor space. In order to attain freedom, know the Self as the 'witness' of all these — the embodiment of pure Consciousness itself.

**Word-by-Word Breakdown:**
* `na` - not
* `pṛthivī` - earth
* `jalaṁ` - water
* `agniḥ` - fire
* `vāyuḥ` - air
* `dyauḥ` - space
* `vā` - or
* `bhavān` - you
* `eṣām` - of these
* `sākṣiṇam` - witness
* `ātmānam` - Self
* `cidrūpam` - embodiment of pure Consciousness
* `viddhi` - know
* `muktaye` - for the sake of freedom

**Philosophical Meaning:**
* **Negation of Materiality:** The verse utilizes the "Neti-Neti" (not this, not that) method to strip away identification with the five great elements (*Pañcamahābhūtas*) that constitute the gross and subtle bodies.
* **The Nature of the Witness (*Sākṣī*):** Liberation (*Mukti*) is defined not as a change in the world, but as a shift in perspective where one recognizes themselves as the detached observer rather than the material object.
* **Pure Consciousness:** The Self is described as *Cidrūpa*, meaning its very form/essence is Consciousness, which remains unaffected by the fluctuations of the material elements it illuminates.

**Memorization Tip:**
* Focus on the rhythmic repetition of "na" (not) in the first line, creating a cadence of systematic negation that leads to the final instruction "viddhi" (know).

#### Ashtavakra Gita 1.13

**Original Sanskrit:**
कूटस्थं बोधमद्वैतमात्मानं परिभावय ।
आभासोऽहं भ्रमं मुक्त्वा भावं बाह्यमथान्तरम् ॥१३॥

**English Transliteration:**
kūṭasthaṁ bodham-advaitam-ātmānaṁ paribhāvaya,
ābhāso'haṁ bhramaṁ muktvā bhāvaṁ bāhyam-athāntaram. (13)

**Complete Translation:**
Having given up all external and internal fluctuations, and the illusion 'I am the reflected Self (ego)', meditate upon the Self as immutable non-dual Consciousness.

**Word-by-Word Breakdown:**
* `kūṭastham` - immutable/changeless
* `bodham` - Consciousness
* `advaitam` - non-dual
* `ātmānam` - Self
* `paribhāvaya` - meditate upon
* `ābhāsaḥ` - reflection/appearance
* `aham` - I
* `bhramam` - illusion/error
* `muktvā` - having given up
* `bhāvam` - fluctuations/states
* `bāhyam` - external
* `atha` - so also
* `antaram` - internal

**Philosophical Meaning:**
* **The Reflected Ego (*Cidābhāsa*):** The primary obstacle is the "reflected I," the ego that identifies with the mind. Realization requires discarding this reflection to find the source light.
* **Immutability (*Kūṭastha*):** Like an anvil (*kūṭa*) that remains stationary while the hammer strikes it, the Self remains changeless despite the world's movements.
* **Non-duality (*Advaita*):** The meditation is not on an object, but on the non-dual essence that leaves no room for "otherness."

**Memorization Tip:**
* Use the word "paribhāvaya" as a phonetic anchor; its four syllables provide a strong conclusion to the first half of the verse.

#### Ashtavakra Gita 2.1

**Original Sanskrit:**
अहो निरञ्जनः शान्तो बोधोऽहं प्रकृतेः परः ।
एतावन्तं अहो कालं मोहेनैव विडम्बितः ॥१॥

**English Transliteration:**
aho nirañjanaḥ śānto bodho'haṁ pṛkṛteḥ paraḥ,
etāvantaṁ aho kālaṁ mohenaiva viḍambitaḥ. (1)

**Complete Translation:**
O! I am the taintless, serene, pure Consciousness, and beyond nature. So long I have spent my days bewildered by delusion.

**Word-by-Word Breakdown:**
* `aho` - O! (expression of wonder)
* `nirañjanaḥ` - taintless/spotless
* `śāntaḥ` - serene/peaceful
* `bodhaḥ` - Consciousness
* `aham` - I am
* `prakṛteḥ` - from nature/matter
* `paraḥ` - beyond
* `etāvantam` - so long/for this much
* `kālam` - time
* `mohena` - by delusion
* `eva` - indeed
* `viḍambitaḥ` - bewildered/mocked

**Philosophical Meaning:**
* **Awakening of Janaka:** This marks the shift from the Guru's instruction to the disciple's realization. The exclamation "Aho!" signifies the sudden, startling nature of Self-discovery.
* **Transcendence of *Prakṛti*:** The Self is recognized as entirely separate from the causal nature/matter (*Prakṛti*) that drives the world of action.
* **The Illusion of Time:** Janaka realizes that his previous suffering was a "dream-play" of delusion (*moha*) that has no standing in his true nature.

**Memorization Tip:**
* Link the two "aho" exclamations in both lines to emphasize the sense of "wonder" and "regret" simultaneously.

#### Ashtavakra Gita 2.4

**Original Sanskrit:**
यथा न तोयतो भिन्नास्तरङ्गाः फेनबुद्बुदाः ।
आत्मनो न तथा भिन्नं विश्वमात्मविनिर्गतम् ॥४॥

**English Transliteration:**
yathā na toyato bhinnās-taraṅgāḥ phena-budbudāḥ,
ātmano na tathā bhinnaṁ viśvam-ātmavinirgatam. (4)

**Complete Translation:**
As waves, foam and bubbles are not different from the waters, so the universe, streaming forth from the Self, is not different from the Self.

**Word-by-Word Breakdown:**
* `yathā` - as
* `na` - not
* `toyataḥ` - from water
* `bhinnāḥ` - different
* `taraṅgāḥ` - waves
* `phena-budbudāḥ` - foam and bubbles
* `ātmanaḥ` - from the Self
* `tathā` - similarly
* `bhinnam` - different
* `viśvam` - universe
* `ātmavinirgatam` - streaming out from the Self

**Philosophical Meaning:**
* **Non-difference of Cause and Effect:** The "Material Cause" (*Upādāna Kāraṇa*) and its effects are essentially one. The universe is a "modification" of the Self, just as waves are a modification of water.
* **Superimposition:** Names and forms (*Nāma-Rūpa*) like "bubble" or "foam" are temporary, but the underlying substance (Water/Self) is permanent.
* **Organic Unity:** The universe is not "created" by an external God but "streams forth" from the Self, maintaining its essential nature as Consciousness throughout.

**Memorization Tip:**
* The phonetic parallel between "taraṅgāḥ" (waves) and "viśvam" (universe) in their respective lines helps map the metaphor to the reality.

#### Ashtavakra Gita 2.8

**Original Sanskrit:**
प्रकाशो मे निजं रूपं नातिरिक्तं ततोऽस्म्यहम् ।
यदा प्रकाशते विश्वं तदाहं भास एव हि ॥८॥

**English Transliteration:**
prakāśo me nijaṁ rūpaṁ nātiriktaṁ tato'smyaham,
yadā prakāśate viśvaṁ tadāhaṁ bhāsa eva hi. (8)

**Complete Translation:**
Light is my very nature; I am nothing other than that Light. When the universe manifests, indeed, it is I alone who shine.

**Word-by-Word Breakdown:**
* `prakāśaḥ` - light/luminosity
* `me` - my
* `nijam` - own
* `rūpam` - nature/form
* `na` - not
* `atiriktam` - different/additional
* `tataḥ` - from that
* `asmi` - am
* `aham` - I
* `yadā` - when
* `prakāśate` - manifests/shines
* `viśvam` - universe
* `tadā` - then
* `bhāse` - shine
* `eva` - alone
* `hi` - indeed

**Philosophical Meaning:**
* **The Self as Subjective Light:** This "Light" is not physical photons but *Caitanya* (Consciousness) which makes awareness possible.
* **Self-Luminosity (*Svayam-Prakāśa*):** The Self does not need a second light to be seen; it is the fundamental illuminator of both the observer and the observed.
* **Cosmic Identity:** When the world is perceived, it is the Consciousness of the Self that provides the "light of existence" to the perceived objects.

**Memorization Tip:**
* The recurring root "kāś" (to shine/appear) in "prakāśa" and "prakāśate" emphasizes the central theme of luminosity.

#### Ashtavakra Gita 2.13

**Original Sanskrit:**
अहो जनं अहो नमो मह्यं यस्य मे नास्ति कञ्चन ।
अथवा यस्य मे सर्वं यद्वाङ्मनसगोचरम् ॥१४॥

**English Transliteration:**
aho ahaṁ namo mahyaṁ yasya me nāsti kiñcana,
athavā yasya me sarvaṁ yad-vāṅ-manasa-gocaram. (14)

**Complete Translation:**
O! Marvellous am I! Salutations to Myself! To me there is nothing, or rather, mine is all that is accessible to speech and mind.

**Word-by-Word Breakdown:**
* `aho` - O! (marvellous)
* `aham` - I
* `namaḥ` - salutations
* `mahyam` - to myself
* `yasya` - for whom
* `me` - my
* `na asti` - is not
* `kiñcana` - anything
* `athavā` - or rather
* `sarvam` - all
* `yat` - which
* `vāṅ-manasa-gocaram` - within the range of speech and mind

**Philosophical Meaning:**
* **Self-Adoration:** This is not egoistic pride but the recognition of the Supreme Self as the only worthy object of worship.
* **The Paradox of All and Nothing:** From the standpoint of the Absolute, nothing exists separate from the Self (Infinite "No-thingness"). From the standpoint of appearance, everything is the Self (Infinite "Everything").
* **Beyond Speech and Mind:** By claiming what is "within the range of speech and mind," Janaka acknowledges that he is the substratum for all concepts and descriptions.

**Memorization Tip:**
* The contrast between "nāsti kiñcana" (nothing exists) and "me sarvam" (all is mine) is the rhythmic heart of this shloka.

#### Ashtavakra Gita 2.22

**Original Sanskrit:**
नाहं देहो न मे देहो जीवो नाहमहं हि चित् ।
अयमेव हि मे बन्ध आसीद् या जीविते स्पृहा ॥२२॥

**English Transliteration:**
nāhaṁ deho na me deho jīvo nāham-ahaṁ hi cit,
ayam-eva hi me bandha āsīd yā jīvite spṛhā. (22)

**Complete Translation:**
I am not the body. Nor have I a body. I am not a being (ego). I am pure Consciousness. That I had desired to live — this indeed was my bondage.

**Word-by-Word Breakdown:**
* `na` - not
* `aham` - I
* `dehaḥ` - body
* `me` - my
* `jīvaḥ` - finite self/ego
* `hi` - indeed
* `cit` - Consciousness
* `ayam` - this
* `eva` - alone
* `bandhaḥ` - bondage
* `āsīt` - was
* `yā` - which
* `jīvite` - for living
* `spṛhā` - desire/thirst

**Philosophical Meaning:**
* **Total Disidentification:** Janaka negates both "I am the body" (identification) and "the body is mine" (possession).
* **Bondage as Desire:** Bondage is not a physical chain but the psychological "will to live" or "thirst for individual existence" (*spṛhā*).
* **The Nature of Cit:** By declaring "aham hi cit," he recognizes himself as the eternal Principle that does not need to "strive" to exist.

**Memorization Tip:**
* The repetition of "na" and "aham" in the first line creates a clear, punching meter that mirrors the forceful nature of the realization.

#### Ashtavakra Gita 3.6

**Original Sanskrit:**
आस्थितः परमाद्वैतं मोक्षार्थेऽपि व्यवस्थितः ।
आश्चर्यं कामवशगो विकलः केलिशिक्षया ॥६॥

**English Transliteration:**
āsthitaḥ paramādvaitaṁ mokṣārthe'pi vyavasthitaḥ,
āścaryaṁ kāmavaśago vikalaḥ keliśikṣayā. (6)

**Complete Translation:**
It is strange indeed, that one abiding in the transcendent non-duality and set for the goal of Liberation should yet come under the sway of lust and be distraught by sexual habits.

**Word-by-Word Breakdown:**
* `āsthitaḥ` - abiding/established
* `parama-advaitam` - supreme non-duality
* `mokṣa-arthe` - for the goal of Liberation
* `api` - even
* `vyavasthitaḥ` - fixed/determined
* `āścaryam` - strange/wonderful (ironic)
* `kāma-vaśagaḥ` - under the sway of lust
* `vikalaḥ` - weakened/distraught
* `keli-śikṣayā` - by sexual habits/playful indulgence

**Philosophical Meaning:**
* **Aṣṭāvakra’s Scolding:** The Master points out the hypocrisy of intellectual knowledge vs. actual transformation.
* **The Trap of Vāsanās:** Even with an intellectual grasp of Advaita, deep-seated latent tendencies (*Vāsanās*) can pull a seeker back into sensory bondage.
* **Consistency in Realization:** True realization must manifest as total dispassion (*Vairāgya*); otherwise, it is mere lip-service.

**Memorization Tip:**
* Notice the contrast between "āsthitaḥ" (established) and "vikalaḥ" (distraught) to remember the spiritual warning.

#### Ashtavakra Gita 4.11

**Original Sanskrit:**
मायामात्रमिदं विश्वं पश्यन् विगतकौतुकः ।
अपि सन्निहिते मृत्यौ कथं त्रस्यति धीरधीः ॥११॥

**Complete Translation:**
Realising this universe as a mere illusion and having lost all zest in life, how can even such a man of poised intellect fear the approach of death?

**Word-by-Word Breakdown:**
* `māyā-mātram` - mere illusion
* `idam` - this
* `viśvam` - universe
* `paśyan` - seeing
* `vigata-kautukaḥ` - all zest/curiosity gone
* `api` - even
* `sannihite` - approaching/near
* `mṛtyau` - in death
* `katham` - how
* `trasyati` - fears
* `dhīradhīḥ` - a man of poised intellect

**Philosophical Meaning:**
* **World as Māyā:** If the world is a dream, the events within it (including death) are equally illusory.
* **Loss of Zest (*Vigata-kautuka*):** This does not mean depression, but the end of "hunger" for transient sensory fulfillment.
* **Fearlessness:** Death is only a threat to the body-ego; to the one who knows themselves as the Infinite, death is merely the end of a projection.

**Memorization Tip:**
* Focus on the compound "dhīradhīḥ" at the end, which phonetically emphasizes the steadiness of the realized mind.

#### Ashtavakra Gita 4.14

**Original Sanskrit:**
अन्तस्त्यक्तकषायस्य निर्द्वन्द्वस्य निराशिषः ।
यदृच्छयागतो भोगो न दुःखाय न तुष्टये ॥१४॥

**English Transliteration:**
antastyakta-kaṣāyasya nirdvandvasya nirāśiṣaḥ,
yadṛcchayāgato bhogo na duḥkhāya na tuṣṭaye. (14)

**Complete Translation:**
He who has given up all worldly passions from his mind, who is beyond the pairs of opposites and who is free from desires, to him objects of enjoyment, unexpectedly reaching him, can cause neither pleasure nor pain.

**Word-by-Word Breakdown:**
* `antaḥ-tyakta-kaṣāyasya` - one who has inwardly given up worldly passions
* `nirdvandvasya` - one beyond pairs of opposites
* `nirāśiṣaḥ` - free from desires/expectations
* `yadṛcchayā` - unexpectedly/by chance
* `āgataḥ` - reached
* `bhogaḥ` - enjoyment/objects
* `na` - not
* `duḥkhāya` - for pain
* `tuṣṭaye` - for pleasure

**Philosophical Meaning:**
* **Equanimity (*Samatva*):** The realized sage does not seek objects, nor does he forcefully reject them if they arrive by "chance" (*Yadṛcchā*).
* **Internal Renunciation:** The focus is on the mind (*Antaḥ*) being free of passions (*Kaṣāya*), regardless of external circumstances.
* **Neutrality:** Because the sage finds bliss in the Self, external objects lose their power to modulate his internal state.

**Memorization Tip:**
* The first line consists of three long descriptive compounds ending in "-asya," creating a repetitive rhythm that defines the sage's character.

#### Ashtavakra Gita 5.1

**Original Sanskrit:**
न ते सङ्गोऽस्ति केनापि किं शुद्धस्त्यक्तुमिच्छसि ।
सङ्घातविलयं कुर्वन्नेवमेव लयं व्रज ॥१॥

**English Transliteration:**
na te saṅgo'sti kenāpi kiṁ śuddhastyaktum-icchasi,
saṅghāta-vilayaṁ kurvannevam-eva layaṁ vraja. (1)

**Complete Translation:**
You have no contact with anything whatsoever. Pure as you are, what do you want to renounce? Having dissolved the body-complex, enter into 'laya' — the state of dissolution.

**Word-by-Word Breakdown:**
* `na` - not
* `te` - your
* `saṅgaḥ` - contact/attachment
* `asti` - is
* `kenāpi` - with anything
* `kim` - what
* `śuddhaḥ` - pure
* `tyaktum` - to renounce
* `icchasi` - wish
* `saṅghāta-vilayam` - dissolution of body-complex
* `kurvan` - effecting
* `evam` - thus
* `layaṁ` - state of dissolution
* `vraja` - attain/enter

**Philosophical Meaning:**
* **The Futility of Renunciation:** Renunciation implies that you were once "attached" to something. Aṣṭāvakra argues that the Self is ever-unattached (*Asaṅga*), so renunciation is an intellectual error.
* **Dissolving the Body-Complex:** Liberation is the dissolution (*Laya*) of the false identification with the "aggregate" (*Saṅghāta*) of senses and mind.
* **Natural State:** "Laya" here refers to returning the mind to its source, where it no longer perceives duality.

**Memorization Tip:**
* The phrase "evam-eva layaṁ vraja" acts as a rhythmic command to end the shloka.

#### Ashtavakra Gita 8.1

**Original Sanskrit:**
तदा बन्धो यदा चित्तं किञ्चिद्वाञ्छति शोचति ।
किञ्चिन्मुञ्चति गृह्णाति किञ्चिद्धृष्यति कुप्यति ॥१॥

**English Transliteration:**

kiñcin-muñcati gṛhṇāti kiñcid-dhṛśyati kupyati. (1)

**Complete Translation:**
It is bondage when the mind desires or grieves at anything; does reject or accept anything; does feel happy or angry at anything.

**Word-by-Word Breakdown:**
* `tadā` - then
* `bandhaḥ` - bondage
* `yadā` - when
* `cittam` - mind
* `kiñcit` - anything
* `vāñchati` - desires
* `śocati` - grieves
* `muñcati` - rejects
* `gṛhṇāti` - accepts
* `hṛśyati` - feels happy
* `kupyati` - feels angry

**Philosophical Meaning:**
* **Psychological Definition of Bondage:** Bondage is not external; it is any movement of the mind (*Citta*) towards or away from objects.
* **The Pairs of Opposites:** Desire/Grief, Rejection/Acceptance, Joy/Anger — these fluctuations define the "unfree" state.
* **Agitation as Prison:** Any "vibration" in the mind that seeks to change the present moment is a state of slavery.

**Memorization Tip:**
* The verbs come in pairs in the second line (muñcati-gṛhṇāti, hṛśyati-kupyati), making it very easy to chant rhythmically.

#### Ashtavakra Gita 8.3

* **Objectivity:** The world continues to be perceived, but the "attachment" to the perception is removed.

**Memorization Tip:**
* Note the direct contrast between "saktaṁ" and "asaktaṁ" as the pivoting point of the shloka.

#### Ashtavakra Gita 9.3

**Original Sanskrit:**
अनित्यं सर्वमेवेदं तापत्रयदूषितम् ।
असारं निन्दितं हेयमिति निश्चित्य शाम्यति ॥३॥

anityaṁ sarvam-evedaṁ tāpa-tritaya-dūṣitam,

**Complete Translation:**
The Man of Wisdom becomes serene by realising that this world indeed is transient, filthy with the triple misery, worthless, contemptible and as something to be rejected.

**Word-by-Word Breakdown:**
* `anityam` - transient/temporary
* `sarvam` - all
* `tāpa-tritaya-dūṣitam` - vitiated by triple misery (Adhyātmika, Adhibhautika, Adhidaivika)
* `asāram` - worthless/without essence
* `ninditam` - contemptible/blameworthy
* `heyam` - rejectable
* `niścitya` - knowing for certain
* `śāmyati` - becomes serene

**Philosophical Meaning:**
* **The Logic of Serenity:** Peace (*Śānti*) is the logical result of an objective evaluation of the world's nature.
* **The Triple Misery:** Recognizing that life in the relative plane is inherently painful (misery from self, others, and nature) leads to dispassion.
* **Certainty (*Niścitya*):** Realization is not a vague hope but a "definite conclusion" of the intellect.

**Memorization Tip:**
* The list of adjectives (anityam, asāram, ninditam, heyam) flows together to build a case for the final word, "śāmyati."

#### Ashtavakra Gita 10.1

**Original Sanskrit:**
विहाय वैरिणं काममर्थं चानर्थसङ्कुलम् ।
धर्ममप्येतयोर्हेतुं सर्वत्रानादरं कुरु ॥१॥

**English Transliteration:**
vihāya vairiṇaṁ kāmam-arthaṁ cānartha-saṅkulam,
dharmam-apyetayor-hetuṁ sarvatrānādaraṁ kuru. (1)

**Complete Translation:**
Having given up 'desire' (kāma) which is the enemy, 'wealth' (artha) which is attended with mischief, and 'piety' (dharma) which is the cause of these two, cultivate indifference to everything.

**Word-by-Word Breakdown:**
* `vihāya` - having given up
* `vairiṇam` - enemy
* `kāmam` - desire
* `artham` - wealth
* `anartha-saṅkulam` - full of mischief/evil
* `dharmam` - piety/merit
* `etayoḥ` - of these two
* `hetum` - cause
* `sarvatra` - everywhere
* `anādaram` - indifference/disregard
* `kuru` - do

**Philosophical Meaning:**
* **Transcending the *Puruṣārthas*:** The standard goals of life (Dharma, Artha, Kāma) are seen as obstacles to the fourth goal (Mokṣa).
* **The Root of Evil:** Dharma (ritualistic merit) is rejected here because it creates the desire for Artha and Kāma (heavenly or worldly pleasures).
* **Supreme Indifference:** The instruction "anādaraṁ kuru" is a call to stop valuing the relative world entirely.

**Memorization Tip:**
* The rhythmic balance between "kāmam-arthaṁ" and "anartha-saṅkulam" highlights the wordplay on "Artha."

#### Ashtavakra Gita 12.3

**Original Sanskrit:**
समाध्यासादिविक्षिप्तौ व्यवहारः समाधये ।
एवं विलोक्य नियममेवमेवाहमास्थितः ॥३॥

**English Transliteration:**
samādhyās-ādi-vikṣiptau vyavahāraḥ samādhaye,
evaṁ vilokya niyamam-evam-evāham-āsthitaḥ. (3)

**Complete Translation:**
An effort has to be made for samādhi (concentration) only when there is distraction of the mind due to one's own superimpositions. Seeing this to be the rule, thus do I, therefore, abide in myself.

**Word-by-Word Breakdown:**
* `samādhyāsa` - superimpositions/projections
* `vikṣiptau` - in distractions
* `vyavahāraḥ` - activity/effort
* `samādhaye` - for concentration
* `vilokya` - seeing/observing
* `niyamam` - rule/law
* `āsthitaḥ` - abide in myself

**Philosophical Meaning:**
* **The Paradox of Meditation:** Meditation is a "cure" for a "disease" (distraction). If the disease is recognized as illusory, the cure is no longer needed.
* **Abiding in the Self:** Realization is not an "act" of concentration, but the natural state of "abiding" once projections are seen as false.
* **Effort vs. Being:** Janaka shifts from "doing" meditation to "being" the Self.

**Memorization Tip:**
* The second line "evam-evāham-āsthitaḥ" is a recurring refrain in Chapter 12, marking Janaka's steady realization.

#### Ashtavakra Gita 12.4

**Original Sanskrit:**
हेयोपादेयविरहादेवं हर्षविषादयोः ।
अभावादद्य हे ब्रह्मन्नेवमेवाहमास्थितः ॥४॥

**English Transliteration:**
heyopādeya-virahād-evaṁ harṣa-viṣādayोः,
abhāvād-adya he brahmannevam-evāham-āsthitaḥ. (4)

**Complete Translation:**
Finding nothing as acceptable and nothing as rejectable and having neither joy nor sorrow, 'O Brahman!' thus do I, therefore, now abide in myself.

**Word-by-Word Breakdown:**
* `heya` - rejectable
* `upādeya` - acceptable
* `virahāt` - in the absence of
* `harṣa-viṣādayoḥ` - of joy and sorrow
* `abhāvāt` - because of absence
* `adya` - today/now
* `he brahman` - O Brahman (addressing the Guru)

**Philosophical Meaning:**
* **Beyond Value Judgments:** When the "I" is gone, the "good/bad" and "joy/sorrow" labels attached by the intellect disappear.
* **Addressing the Guru as Brahman:** Janaka recognizes the Guru as the Absolute, reflecting his own newfound identity.
* **The Present Moment (*Adya*):** Realization is always "now," in the absence of past regrets or future desires.

**Memorization Tip:**
* Group "heyopādeya" and "harṣa-viṣādayoḥ" together as the pairs of opposites Janaka has transcended.

#### Ashtavakra Gita 12.7

**Original Sanskrit:**
अचिन्त्यं चिन्त्यमानोऽपि चिन्तारूपं भजत्यसौ ।
त्यक्त्वा तद्भावनं तस्मादेवमेवाहमास्थितः ॥७॥

**English Transliteration:**
acintyaṁ cintyamāno'pi cintārūpaṁ bhajatyasau,
tyaktvā tadbhāvanaṁ tasmād-evam-evāham-āsthitaḥ. (7)

**Complete Translation:**
Thinking on the unthinkable One, one resorts only to a form of (one's own) thought. Therefore, giving up that thought, thus do I, indeed, abide in myself.

**Word-by-Word Breakdown:**
* `acintyam` - the unthinkable
* `cintyamānaḥ` - thinking
* `api` - even
* `cintā-rūpam` - a form of thought
* `bhajati` - resorts to/worships
* `tyaktvā` - giving up
* `tad-bhāvanam` - that thought/conception

**Philosophical Meaning:**
* **The Limit of Thought:** The Self cannot be an "object" of thought because it is the "subject" that makes thought possible.
* **The Subtle Trap:** Even "thinking about Brahman" is still just a thought (*Vṛtti*). True realization occurs when even that last spiritual thought is dropped.
* **Silent Abidance:** Realization is the silence that remains when the "thought of the unthinkable" is surrendered.

**Memorization Tip:**
* The contrast between "acintyam" (unthinkable) and "cintārūpam" (form of thought) illustrates the intellectual futility Janaka has escaped.
